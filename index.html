How it works

1. Build a picture sentence

Who, does what, to what, where. Big emoji tiles, no reading required. A 4-year-old can do it alone.

2. Tap Make. Play your game.

Thirty seconds later there is a 3D game that your kid made. One button to play, so little hands can do it in the car.

3. Earn upgrades by learning

Locked tiles show a padlock. Count to 10, sound out a word, or solve a puzzle to unlock. Never a checkout.

Every upgrade is a challenge, not a purchase

Other kids' apps sell coins. Game Maker asks your kid to count to 100, read a word, or solve a puzzle. New worlds, new heroes, theme songs, and even Mom and Dad's recorded voices as game characters are all earned.

You pay once for the toy. There is nothing else to buy, and there never will be.

Questions

Is this real?

The software is real and being tested with kids right now. The toy is a working design that isn't manufactured yet. Joining the list costs nothing and helps us decide how many to build.

What ages?

Three to seven. Pre-readers can build games alone because everything is pictures. Challenges scale with age.

Really no in-app purchases?

Really. The business model is the $99 toy. Upgrades are unlocked by learning, and that will never change.

Does it need Wi-Fi?

Only to bake new games at home. Playing works fully offline, which is the whole point for car rides.
